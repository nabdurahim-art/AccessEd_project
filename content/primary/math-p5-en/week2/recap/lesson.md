# Week 2 recap: collecting and showing data

## The three big ideas

**Idea one: kinds of data.** Quantitative data uses numbers. Qualitative data uses describing words. Discrete data is counted. Continuous data is measured.

**Idea two: tally charts and bar charts.** Tally marks come in bundles of 5. The total for each group is its frequency. In a bar chart the height of each bar is the frequency.

**Idea three: line graphs.** A line graph shows change over time. Up means rising, down means falling, flat means no change.

## Real-life challenge: the soda seller

A shopkeeper counts the sodas she sells each day.

- Monday: 12
- Tuesday: 15
- Wednesday: 9
- Thursday: 18
- Friday: 21

Answer these before you read on.

- Is this data discrete or continuous?
- On which day did she sell the most?
- How many sodas did she sell in the whole week?
- She wants to see how her sales change through the week. Which graph should she draw?

## Check your work

- Discrete. She counts sodas in whole numbers.
- Friday, with 21 sodas.
- 12 + 15 + 9 + 18 + 21 = 75 sodas.
- A line graph, because it shows change over time.
