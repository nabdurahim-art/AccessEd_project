# Week 1 recap: points, lines and angles

## The three big ideas

**Idea one: lines and types of angles.** A segment has two endpoints, a ray has one, a line has none. Angles are acute, right, obtuse, straight or reflex.

**Idea two: angle facts.** Angles on a straight line add up to 180°. Angles at a point add up to 360°. Vertically opposite, corresponding and alternate angles are equal. Co-interior angles add up to 180°.

**Idea three: polygons.** The interior angles add up to (n - 2) × 180°. The exterior angles add up to 360°. In a regular polygon, divide by n to find one angle.

## Real-life challenge: the tiler

A tiler wants to cover a floor with regular hexagon tiles, leaving no gaps.

- First, find each interior angle of a regular hexagon.
- Then, three tiles meet at one point on the floor. Add the three angles that meet there. Is there a gap?
- Last, could the tiler use regular pentagon tiles in the same way? Show why or why not.

Work it out before you read on.

## Check your work

- Hexagon: (6 - 2) × 180 = 720, and 720 ÷ 6 = 120°.
- Three tiles: 120 + 120 + 120 = 360°. Angles at a point add up to 360°, so there is no gap.
- Pentagon: (5 - 2) × 180 = 540, and 540 ÷ 5 = 108°. Three tiles give 324°, which leaves a gap of 36°. Four tiles give 432°, which is too much. So pentagons do not work.

You used all three lessons in one problem. Well done for trying.
