# Week 1 recap: classifying living things

## The three big ideas

**Idea one: classification and keys.** Classification is sorting organisms into groups by their features. A dichotomous key gives two choices at each step and leads you to a name.

**Idea two: levels and names.** The seven levels are kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species. Every organism has a scientific name with two parts: the genus with a capital letter, then the species with a small letter.

**Idea three: five kingdoms.** Animalia, Plantae, Fungi, Protoctista and Monera. They differ in body structure, how they get food and how they reproduce.

## Real-life challenge: the old bread

A learner leaves a piece of bread in a cupboard for a week. A soft growth appears on it, white at first and then black. Under a hand lens she sees thin threads with tiny round heads full of spores. The growth is not green and it does not move.

Answer these before you read on.

- Is the growth a living thing? Give one reason.
- To which kingdom does it belong? Give two reasons.
- She writes its name as "rhizopus Stolonifer". Correct her writing.
- In that name, which word is the genus?

## Check your work

- Yes. It grows and it reproduces.
- Fungi. It does not photosynthesise, and it reproduces by spores.
- The correct name is *Rhizopus stolonifer*: capital letter for the genus, small letter for the species.
- Rhizopus is the genus.
