# Audio reading scripts

Record each lesson in a quiet room, at a calm speed, with a short pause between paragraphs.
Name each recording exactly as shown, put it in the `recordings/` folder, then run `python3 tools/import_audio.py`.

## Primary - Maths - Week 1: Numbers and shapes

Counting to 100.

Numbers help us count things: goats, books, steps.

### Adding and taking away
File name: `primary-math-en-week1-m2.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Adding and taking away.

Adding means putting numbers together. The sign is +.

Taking away means removing some. The sign is -.

Adding.

3 plus 2 equals 5.

Three mangoes and two mangoes make five mangoes.

Taking away.

5 minus 2 equals 3.

You have five eggs. You use two. Three eggs are left.

You can use your fingers, small stones or sticks to help you.

Try it: what is 4 plus 3?

### Shapes around us
File name: `primary-math-en-week1-m3.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Shapes around us.

Shapes are everywhere. Look around you.

A circle is round. It has no corners. A coin and a plate are circles.

A square has four sides. All four sides are the same length.

A rectangle has four sides. Two sides are long and two are short. A door and a book are rectangles.

A triangle has three sides and three corners.

Try it: find one circle and one rectangle in your home.

## Primary - Science - Week 1: Living things and our senses

### Living and non-living things
File name: `primary-science-en-week1-m1.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Living and non-living things.

Living things are alive. People, goats, birds and trees are living things.

A living thing can:

grow.

need food and water.

have babies or make seeds.

Non-living things are not alive. A stone, a chair, a bicycle and a cup are non-living.

A tree does not walk, but it grows and makes seeds. So a tree is living.

Try it: name three living things and three non-living things near you.

### Our five senses
File name: `primary-science-en-week1-m2.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Our five senses.

We use our body to learn about the world. We have five senses.

Sight: we see with our eyes.

Hearing: we hear with our ears.

Smell: we smell with our nose.

Taste: we taste with our tongue.

Touch: we feel with our skin.

Stay safe.

Never taste something if you do not know what it is.

Never look straight at the sun.

Do not touch a hot pot.

Try it: close your eyes and listen. How many sounds can you hear?

### Plants and what they need
File name: `primary-science-en-week1-m3.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Plants and what they need.

Plants are living things. To grow well, a plant needs:

water.

sunlight.

air.

good soil.

Parts of a plant.

The roots hold the plant in the ground and take in water.

The stem carries water up to the leaves.

The leaves use sunlight to make food for the plant.

The flower makes seeds and fruit.

Try it: put a bean seed in wet soil and watch it grow for a week.

My family.

## Secondary - Life skills - Week 1: Health and safety

### Washing your hands
File name: `secondary-lifeskills-en-week1-m1.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Washing your hands.

Washing hands with soap stops germs from spreading. It helps prevent sickness such as diarrhoea.

When to wash.

Before you eat or prepare food.

After using the toilet.

After touching animals.

When your hands look dirty.

How to wash.

Wet your hands with clean water.

Rub soap on your palms, the backs of your hands, between your fingers and under your nails.

Scrub for at least 20 seconds.

Rinse well and dry with a clean cloth or in the air.

### Saving a little each week
File name: `secondary-lifeskills-en-week1-m2.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Saving a little each week.

Saving means keeping some money for later instead of spending all of it.

Small amounts grow: 200 Rwandan francs every week for 10 weeks is 2,000 Rwandan francs.

How to start.

Choose a goal, for example school fees or a small business.

Decide an amount you can keep every week.

Keep it in a safe place.

Write each saving in a notebook.

Needs and wants.

A need is something you must have, like food. A want is something nice to have, like sweets.

### Staying safe online
File name: `secondary-lifeskills-en-week1-m3.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Staying safe online.

Keep private things private. Never share:

Your password.

Your ID number or home address.

Any code or PIN sent to your phone.

No real bank or mobile money company will ask you for your PIN or a code.

A strong password.

At least 8 characters.

Letters and numbers mixed.

Not your name or birth date.

If something feels wrong.

Do not click links in messages from people you do not know. Tell a trusted adult.

## Secondary - Literacy basics - Week 1: Letters and sounds

### Vowels and consonants
File name: `secondary-literacy-en-week1-m1.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Vowels and consonants.

The English alphabet has 26 letters. Five of them are vowels: A, E, I, O, U. All the other letters are consonants.

Why it matters.

Almost every word has at least one vowel. When you read a word, look for the vowel first.

cat has the vowel a.

pen has the vowel e.

sun has the vowel u.

Try it.

Say each vowel out loud: a, e, i, o, u. Then say the word bag. Which letter is the vowel?

### Building short words
File name: `secondary-literacy-en-week1-m2.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Building short words.

Join letter sounds together to make a word. Say each sound, then say them fast.

b plus a plus g equals bag.

m plus a plus p equals map.

p plus i plus g equals pig.

h plus o plus t equals hot.

Word families.

Change only the first letter and you get a new word. These words all end in -at:

cat.

hat.

mat.

sat.

Practise: say each word, then write it on paper.

### Reading a short sentence
File name: `secondary-literacy-en-week1-m3.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Reading a short sentence.

A sentence tells one complete idea. It starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop.

Example: The sun is hot.

How to read it.

Read slowly, one word at a time.

Point at each word with your finger.

Read it again, a little faster.

Write your own.

Copy this sentence and fill in your name: My name is your name.

## Secondary - Everyday maths - Week 1: Numbers and money

### Counting and place value
File name: `secondary-math-en-week1-m1.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Counting and place value.

A number is made of digits. Each digit has a place.

In 47 there are 4 tens and 7 ones. So 47 equals 40 plus 7.

23 equals 2 tens and 3 ones.

85 equals 8 tens and 5 ones.

100 equals ten tens.

Counting in tens.

10, 20, 30, 40, 50 ...

Try it: how many tens and ones are in 62?

### Adding and subtracting
File name: `secondary-math-en-week1-m2.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Adding and subtracting.

Adding puts amounts together. Subtracting takes an amount away.

Adding.

25 plus 14 equals 39.

Add the ones: 5 plus 4 equals 9.

Add the tens: 2 plus 1 equals 3.

Answer: 39.

Subtracting.

38 minus 12 equals 26.

Subtract the ones: 8 minus 2 equals 6.

Subtract the tens: 3 minus 1 equals 2.

Answer: 26.

With money.

You have 300 Rwandan francs and earn 200 Rwandan francs: 300 plus 200 equals 500 Rwandan francs.

You have 500 Rwandan francs and spend 150 Rwandan francs: 500 minus 150 equals 350 Rwandan francs.

### Using money and change
File name: `secondary-math-en-week1-m3.m4a` (or .mp3, .wav, .ogg)

Using money and change.

When you buy something, you pay the price. If you give more than the price, the seller gives you change.

Total cost.

A notebook costs 400 Rwandan francs and a pen costs 250 Rwandan francs.

400 plus 250 equals 650 Rwandan francs.

Change.

You pay with a 1,000 Rwandan francs note for something that costs 700 Rwandan francs.

1,000 minus 700 equals 300 Rwandan francs change.

Check your change.

Always count your change before you walk away.
